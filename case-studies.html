01

Vehicle routing with precedence constraints

ns3084303

A truck visits \(T\) locations in \(T\) days, one per day. At each stop it picks up or drops off a fixed amount of cargo, and its load must stay within its capacity \(Q.\) Choose the visit order that minimizes total driving distance and satisfies the required order between stops.

Original formulation

Sets and indices
Let \(S=\{1,\ldots,T\}\) be the common index set for locations \(i,j\) and visit days \(t,u.\) Let \(S_0=S\cup\{0\}\) be the set of days including the initial state. The set of precedence pairs is \(\mathcal P=\{(3,5),(4,2),(1,6),(2,7)\}.\) For each \((a,b)\in\mathcal P,\) location \(a\) must be visited before location \(b.\)
Parameters
\(T=25\) is the number of locations and visit days, and \(Q=10\) is the vehicle capacity. Let \(a_i\in[-3.5,3.5]\) denote the signed load change at location \(i.\) Let \(d_{ij}\in[1,10]\) denote the travel distance between distinct locations \(i\) and \(j.\) Distances are symmetric, with \(d_{ii}=0.\)

Decision variables

Route arcs

\(x_{ij}\) is 1 if arc \((i,j)\) is selected and 0 otherwise.

\[ \begin{aligned} & x_{ij}\in\{0,1\}, & & \forall\,i,j\in S \end{aligned} \]
Visit indicators

For \(t\in S,\) \(y_{it}\) is 1 if the truck visits location \(i\) on day \(t,\) and 0 otherwise. The entries for day zero are initial markers.

\[ \begin{aligned} & y_{it}\in\{0,1\}, & & \forall\,(i,t)\in S\times S_0 \end{aligned} \]
Load after each day

\(f_t\) records the vehicle load after day \(t.\)

\[ \begin{aligned} & f_t\in\mathbb R, & & \forall\,t\in S_0 \end{aligned} \]

Objective

Minimize the total distance of the selected arcs. An arc contributes \(d_{ij}\) when \(x_{ij}=1.\)

\[ \begin{aligned} \min_{x,y,f}\quad & \sum_{(i,j)\in S\times S} d_{ij}x_{ij} \end{aligned} \]

Constraints

Arc selection

Require at least one selected arc leaving and at least one entering each location.

\[ \begin{aligned} & \sum_{j\in S}x_{ij}\ge 1, & & \forall\,i\in S & \quad\text{(1.1)} \\ & \sum_{i\in S}x_{ij}\ge 1, & & \forall\,j\in S & \quad\text{(1.2)} \end{aligned} \]
Daily assignments

Assign each location to exactly one visit day and each visit day to exactly one location. Fix the day-zero marker \(y_{s,0}\) at 1.

\[ \begin{aligned} & \sum_{t\in S}y_{it}=1, & & \forall\,i\in S & \quad\text{(1.3)} \\ & \sum_{i\in S}y_{it}=1, & & \forall\,t\in S & \quad\text{(1.4)} \\ & y_{s,0}=1 & & & \quad\text{(1.5)} \end{aligned} \]
Route and visit days

If arc \((i,j)\) is selected and the truck visits \(i\) on day \(t-1,\) it must visit \(j\) on day \(t.\)

\[ \begin{aligned} & x_{ij}+y_{i,t-1}\le 1+y_{jt}, & & \forall\,i,j\in S,\quad \forall\,t\in\{2,\ldots,T\} & \quad\text{(1.6)} \end{aligned} \]
Visit order

For each \((a,b)\in\mathcal P,\) compare the two visit days. The daily assignments make the days distinct, so \(a\) must precede \(b.\)

\[ \begin{aligned} & \textcolor{#255ba8}{\sum_{t\in S}t\,y_{at}\le\sum_{t\in S}t\,y_{bt}}, & & \forall\,(a,b)\in\mathcal P & \quad\text{(1.7)} \end{aligned} \]
Vehicle load

Start with zero load, add the signed load change at the location visited each day, and enforce capacity \(Q.\)

\[ \begin{aligned} & f_0=0 & & & \quad\text{(1.8)} \\ & f_t=f_{t-1}+\sum_{i\in S}a_i y_{it}, & & \forall\,t\in S & \quad\text{(1.9)} \\ & f_t\le Q, & & \forall\,t\in S & \quad\text{(1.10)} \end{aligned} \]

Reformulate the route and visit order

Route arcs · Link visits by position

The original formulation (1.6) links each arc to the visit sequence separately for every day. A position-based formulation uses one continuous visit position \(p_i\) per location and links each selected arc directly to its successor position.

Original · One implication per arc and day
\(x_{ij}+y_{i,t-1}\le 1+y_{jt},\) \(\forall\,i,j\in S,\quad t=2,\ldots,T\)
→Same optimal value
Reformulated · Two bounds per arc
\(\displaystyle p_i=\sum_{t\in S}t\,y_{it},\) \(q_i=p_i+1-Ty_{iT}\)
\(\lvert p_j-q_i\rvert\le (T-1)(1-x_{ij}),\) \(\forall\,i,j\in S,\ i\ne j\)
Proof

Equivalence of optimal values. The assignment constraints define a visit order \(v_1,\ldots,v_T\). For \(t<T\), (1.6) permits only the arc from \(v_t\) to \(v_{t+1}\), and (1.1) requires it. Constraint (1.2) then forces the arc from \(v_T\) back to \(v_1\). Deleting any extra arcs from \(v_T\) leaves a feasible cycle and cannot increase the objective because distances are nonnegative.

This cycle satisfies the reformulated bounds: each selected arc has \(p_j=q_i\), since \(q_i\) is the next position with wraparound from \(T\) to 1. For unselected arcs, \(p_j,q_i\in[1,T]\) implies \(\lvert p_j-q_i\rvert\le T-1\). Conversely, the reformulation requires one incoming and one outgoing arc per location, and \(p_j=q_i\) forces those arcs to follow the same cycle. That cycle satisfies (1.1), (1.2), and (1.6) with the same objective value.

The arc-linking block goes from \(T^2(T-1)\) rows to \(2T(T-1)\) bounds and \(T\) position definitions.

Tighten the visit-order constraints

The original constraint (1.7) compares the days on which the truck visits \(a\) and \(b.\)

The reformulation checks the order one day at a time. If the truck has visited \(b\) by day \(t,\) it must have visited \(a\) by day \(t-1.\) Each cumulative sum counts visits to one stop up to the specified day.

Original · Compare visit days
\(\displaystyle\sum_{t\in S}t\,y_{at}\le\sum_{t\in S}t\,y_{bt},\) \(\forall\,(a,b)\in\mathcal P\)
⇔Integer equivalence
Reformulated · Enforce order day by day
\(\displaystyle\sum_{u=1}^{t}y_{bu}\le\sum_{u=1}^{t-1}y_{au},\) \(\forall\,(a,b)\in\mathcal P,\quad \forall\,t\in S\)
Proof

Integer equivalence. Constraints (1.3) and (1.4) assign each stop a distinct visit day, so (1.7) requires \(a\) to precede \(b.\) On the day the truck visits \(b,\) the cumulative constraint requires \(a\) to have been visited by the previous day. Conversely, visiting \(a\) before \(b\) satisfies every cumulative constraint for the pair.

LP strengthening. Summing the daily inequalities and using the unit visit totals in (1.3) gives \(\sum_{t\in S}t\,y_{bt}\ge 1+\sum_{t\in S}t\,y_{at},\quad \forall\,(a,b)\in\mathcal P.\) Constraint (1.7) allows equal weighted average visit days, which the cumulative constraints exclude.

02

Module placement on a grid

ns3025225

The placement problem

Place \(L\) layers with \(B\) modules each on a shared board with \(H\) rows and \(W\) columns, with at most one module per cell. Within each layer, the highest and lowest occupied rows may differ by at most \(s.\)

Each module connects to a fixed port. Choose the cells so that the largest horizontal distance between a module and its port is as small as possible.

Original formulation

Sets and indices
Let \(i\in I=\{1,\ldots,L\}\) index layers and \(j,k\in J=\{1,\ldots,B\}\) index modules within a layer. A cell \((m,n)\in R\times C\) has row \(m\in R=\{1,\ldots,H\}\) and column \(n\in C=\{1,\ldots,W\}.\)
Parameters
The instance has \(L=5\) layers, \(B=65\) modules per layer, \(H=13\) rows and \(W=25\) columns. The maximum difference between module rows in a layer is \(s=3.\) The upper bound on the signed offset is \(D=1.\) The port coordinates are \(a_j=Wj/(B+1),\ \forall\,j\in J.\) The signed offsets from columns to ports are \(d_{jn}=a_j-(n-\tfrac12),\ \forall\,(j,n)\in J\times C.\)

Decision variables

Cell placement

\(p_{ijmn}\) is 1 if module \(j\) of layer \(i\) is assigned to cell \((m,n)\) and 0 otherwise.

\[p_{ijmn}\in\{0,1\},\quad \forall\,(i,j,m,n)\in I\times J\times R\times C\]
Distance bound

\(M\) is a continuous upper bound on every module’s absolute horizontal offset.

\[M\in\mathbb R\]
Derived quantities

In an integer placement, \(q_{ij}\) is the signed offset of the assigned cell and \(y_{ij}\) is its row index.

\[ \begin{aligned} q_{ij} &= \sum_{(m,n)\in R\times C}d_{jn}p_{ijmn}, & & \forall\,(i,j)\in I\times J, \\ y_{ij} &= \sum_{(m,n)\in R\times C}m\,p_{ijmn}, & & \forall\,(i,j)\in I\times J \end{aligned} \]

Objective

Minimizing \(M\) minimizes the largest absolute horizontal offset.

\[\min_{p,M}\quad M\]

Constraints

Cell assignments

Each module uses at most one cell, each cell holds at most one module, and the total number of placements is \(LB.\) Together these conditions assign exactly one cell to each module.

\[ \begin{aligned} & \sum_{(m,n)\in R\times C}p_{ijmn}\le 1, & & \forall\,(i,j)\in I\times J & \quad\text{(2.1)} \\ & \sum_{(i,j)\in I\times J}p_{ijmn}\le 1, & & \forall\,(m,n)\in R\times C & \quad\text{(2.2)} \\ & \sum_{(i,j)\in I\times J}\sum_{(m,n)\in R\times C}p_{ijmn}=LB & & & \quad\text{(2.3)} \end{aligned} \]
Distance bounds

For the selected cell \((m,n),\) the two inequalities require \(M\ge d_{jn}\) and \(M\ge -d_{jn}.\) Together they give \(M\ge |d_{jn}|.\)

\[ \begin{aligned} & \textcolor{#255ba8}{M\ge d_{jn}p_{ijmn}}, & & \forall\,(i,j,m,n)\in I\times J\times R\times C & \quad\text{(2.4)} \\ & \textcolor{#255ba8}{M\ge -d_{jn}p_{ijmn}}, & & \forall\,(i,j,m,n)\in I\times J\times R\times C & \quad\text{(2.5)} \end{aligned} \]
Signed offset limit

Require \(q_{ij}\le D,\) limiting how far to the left of its port a module can be placed.

\[ \begin{aligned} & q_{ij}\le D, & & \forall\,(i,j)\in I\times J & \quad\text{(2.6)} \end{aligned} \]
Layer height

Limit the difference between the row indices of any two modules in the same layer to \(s.\)

\[ \begin{aligned} & \textcolor{#255ba8}{-s\le y_{ij}-y_{ik}\le s}, & & \forall\,i\in I,\quad \forall\,j,k\in J,\ j<k & \quad\text{(2.7)} \end{aligned} \]
Row limit

A module in layer \(i\) can only use rows numbered at most \(is.\)

\[ \begin{aligned} & m\,p_{ijmn}\le is, & & \forall\,(i,j,m,n)\in I\times J\times R\times C & \quad\text{(2.8)} \end{aligned} \]

Tighten bounds and reduce the model size

Distance · One bound per module

LLM4MOR combines the cell-level bounds on \(M\) in (2.4) and (2.5) into one bound per module.

Original · Two bounds per candidate cell
\(M\ge d_{jn}p_{ijmn},\quad M\ge -d_{jn}p_{ijmn},\) \(\forall\,(i,j,m,n)\in I\times J\times R\times C\)
⇔Integer equivalence
Reformulated · Sum over cells
\(\displaystyle M\ge\sum_{(m,n)\in R\times C}|d_{jn}|p_{ijmn},\) \(\forall\,(i,j)\in I\times J\)
Proof

Integer equivalence. Constraints (2.1) and (2.3) require exactly one selected cell per module. Only that cell contributes to the sum, so both formulations require \(M\) to be at least its absolute offset.

LP strengthening. Each term \(|d_{jn}|p_{ijmn}\) is nonnegative, so the sum implies both original bounds for every cell. The sum exceeds the largest individual term whenever at least two cells contribute positive terms.

Height · One shared interval per layer

The original constraints (2.7) compare the row positions of every pair of modules in a layer.

The reformulation considers the layer as a whole. If all its module rows fit in an interval of length \(s,\) any two rows differ by at most \(s.\) The bounds below place each row in layer \(i\) between the shared endpoints \(b_i\) and \(b_i+s.\)

Original · Compare module pairs
\(-s\le y_{ij}-y_{ik}\le s,\) \(\forall\,i\in I,\quad \forall\,j,k\in J,\ j<k\)
⇔Equivalent row values
Reformulated · Bound each module row
\(b_i\le y_{ij}\le b_i+s,\) \(\forall\,(i,j)\in I\times J\)
\(b_i\in\mathbb R,\) \(\forall\,i\in I\)
Proof

The pairwise bounds (2.7) hold exactly when \(\max_{j\in J} y_{ij}-\min_{j\in J} y_{ij}\le s,\quad \forall\,i\in I.\) In that case, choosing \(b_i=\min_{j\in J} y_{ij},\quad \forall\,i\in I,\) gives a valid interval. Conversely, any two row values in the same interval differ by at most \(s.\)

Placement domain · Use grid capacity to tighten row bounds

The cell capacity constraint (2.2), the within-layer span limit (2.7), and the layer row cap (2.8) together identify rows that no module in a layer can use. LLM4MOR omits the corresponding binary placement variables when building the model.

Proof

In any feasible integer placement, let \(u_i=\max_{1\le h\le i,\ j\in J}y_{hj}\) be the highest row occupied by the first \(i\) layers. These layers occupy \(iB\) distinct cells, and rows 1 through \(u_i\) contain \(Wu_i\) cells. Thus

\[iB\le Wu_i\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u_i\ge r_i:=\left\lceil\frac{iB}{W}\right\rceil.\]

If \(r_i>(i-1)s\), the row caps in (2.8) prevent earlier layers from reaching row \(r_i\). Layer \(i\) must therefore attain \(u_i\). The span bound (2.7) then gives, for each module \(j\) in that layer,

\[y_{ij}\ge u_i-s\ge r_i-s.\]

Use \(\ell_i=\max(1,r_i-s)\) in this case and \(\ell_i=1\) otherwise. Together with the original row cap and board height, this gives the candidate row set

\[R_i=\{m\in R:\ell_i\le m\le\min(H,is)\}.\]

Declare \(p_{ijmn}\) only for rows \(m\in R_i\). Every omitted variable is zero in all feasible integer placements, so the reduction preserves those placements.

Applying them after filtering cells by the row cap and signed offset limit cuts the remaining placement variables by 51.2%.
