# Pipe-Routing Model and Reformulation

## 1. Background and interpretation

The model represents the routing of one industrial pipe between two fixed connection points in a three-dimensional plant layout. Coordinates use a discretization unit of **200 mm**. The route consists of axis-aligned straight segments joined by right-angle bends.

The fixed layout contains equipment modules, existing pipe segments, support zones, a pipe rack, and preferred rack support levels. A feasible route must:

- Respect obstacle clearances, accounting for the pipe radius.
- Follow prescribed directions and length limits at its connections.
- Place intermediate nodes in permitted support zones.
- Satisfy cubic-length flexibility conditions, apparently intended to represent piping flexibility requirements.
- Minimize construction costs associated with pipe length, bends, and supports.

The model permits up to nine segments. Unused segments form a trailing sequence of zero-length segments at the destination. Although the data contain a rack-traversal flag, the corresponding constraint is commented out in the original model; the reformulation therefore does **not** require rack traversal.

## 2. Original model structure

### Variables

The original independent decision variables comprise **151 integer scalar entries**, including fixed entries:

| Variables | Entries | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Node coordinates | 30 | Ten three-dimensional nodes; the six endpoint coordinates are fixed |
| `nXLeg1`, `nXLeg2` | 60 | Lower and upper corners of radius-expanded segment boxes |
| `nLLeg` | 30 | Segment lengths along each coordinate axis |
| `nDirLeg` | 10 | Direction: zero for an unused segment, otherwise one of six signed axes |
| `fExistLeg`, `fLastLeg` | 20 | Segment-existence and final-segment indicators |
| `obj` | 1 | Objective value |

The segment arrays have ten slots: nine possible segments and one padding slot fixed to zero. Additional defined arrays express obstacle separation, support-zone membership, support costs, and sums of cubed lengths. These source-level counts are not counts of the variables generated by MiniZinc flattening.

### Constraints

The main constraint groups are:

- **Segment geometry.** Expanded segment boxes enclose their endpoint coordinates. Their widths, minus twice the pipe radius, equal the coordinate lengths. Direction constraints force the selected coordinate length to equal the signed endpoint displacement and force all other coordinate lengths to zero.
- **Route sequence.** At least the first three segments exist. Exactly one segment is marked final, and the existence equations force unused segments to follow it.
- **Orthogonality.** Consecutive existing segments cannot use the same coordinate axis, excluding both straight continuation and immediate reversal.
- **Connection restrictions.** The initial and final segments have specified directions and nozzle-length limits. The final segment ends at the fixed destination.
- **Obstacle avoidance.** Segment boxes must be separated from equipment and existing pipes along at least one coordinate axis, with specified safety distances. Explicit exemptions allow connection to the designated endpoint modules.
- **Support requirements.** Intermediate nodes must belong to a support zone. Membership is evaluated at the node center horizontally and at the pipe bottom vertically. These requirements also apply to the intermediate node slots associated with unused segments.
- **Flexibility.** For each coordinate axis, the sum of cubed segment lengths in the other two axes must exceed a coefficient times each participating segment length. The coefficients depend on endpoint separation, length units, and the supplied flexibility factor.

Inter-pipe separation constraints are present in the source, but generate no restrictions for this single-pipe instance. The model does not impose self-avoidance between nonadjacent segments of the same pipe.

### Objective

The model **minimizes** a scaled construction-cost expression. Its coefficients use integer division by 100 followed by adding one:

- **131** per unit of pipe length.
- **1,301** per existing segment after the first, representing bend cost.
- **51** per active intermediate node outside preferred support levels, or **1** in a preferred level for this instance.

The reformulation preserves these coefficients and the minimization direction exactly.

## 3. Reformulation and validity

### Exact discrete reformulation of cubic lengths

Repeated variable cubic expressions were replaced by a discrete length-selection formulation shared across the coordinate axes of each segment.

For each segment, binary selectors choose one total length from the bounded integer range. Linear equations associate that selected length with its precomputed cube. Auxiliary coordinate-cube variables sum to the selected cube and are forced to zero on inactive axes.

**Why this is exact:** the original direction constraints allow at most one nonzero coordinate length. Existing segments must have a positive length, while nonexistent segments have all lengths zero. Consequently, the total segment length equals its only nonzero coordinate length. Assigning its cube to the selected axis therefore reproduces every original coordinate cube exactly—not a piecewise-linear approximation.

The selector range is reduced using segment-specific upper bounds and the original minimum active length. Values outside those bounds cannot occur in an original feasible solution.

This formulation replaces nonlinear integer expressions with explicit mixed-integer linear constraints suitable for HiGHS.

### Coordinate and segment bound strengthening

Coordinate-length upper bounds were derived from the declared endpoint-coordinate domains and the original segment-length limit. Initial and final-possible segments also use their nozzle-specific limits.

**Why these bounds are valid:** the original enclosure, width, and direction constraints together make a coordinate length equal to the absolute endpoint displacement on the selected axis. Such a displacement cannot exceed the span of the endpoint domains. The nozzle bounds are already imposed by the original model.

These bounds tighten both length activation constraints and the auxiliary cube formulation. Additional inequalities bound each cube between the minimum-active-length squared times the length and the applicable maximum-length squared times the length. They follow directly from \(m \leq L \leq U\), which implies \(m^2L \leq L^3 \leq U^2L\); they also hold at \(L=0\).

### Bound-based geometric preprocessing

An obstacle test is omitted only when the segment’s coordinate domains prove that its entire radius-expanded box is separated from that obstacle along at least one axis, including safety clearance.

**Why this is valid:** the original model explicitly bounds the segment-box corners using the endpoint domains and pipe radius. If those bounds already imply one separation inequality, the obstacle disjunction is true for every feasible assignment.

Support-zone membership is similarly fixed to false when a node’s coordinate domain cannot intersect the zone on some axis, using the original vertical pipe-bottom offset. Such membership is geometrically impossible, so this simplification removes no feasible route.

### Linearization of support costs

Products between segment-existence indicators and support-category indicators were replaced by Boolean conjunctions multiplied by constant costs.

**Why this is exact:** both factors are binary. Their product equals one exactly when both conditions hold. The original preferred-level membership expression and its binary domain are retained, so the support categories and costs are unchanged.

### Endpoint-distance cuts

The reformulation adds a lower bound on total travel along each axis equal to the absolute endpoint displacement.

**Why this is valid:** segment displacements telescope to the fixed endpoint displacement, while each coordinate length is the absolute value of its segment displacement. The triangle inequality therefore gives
\[
\sum_{\ell} L_{\ell k}\geq |\Delta_k|.
\]

A stronger cut applies when the prescribed initial direction points away from the destination. In that case, total travel along the axis must be at least the endpoint distance plus twice the initial segment length.

**Why this is valid:** the initial movement away from the destination must subsequently be canceled before the route can cover the required net displacement. Both the outward movement and its cancellation contribute to total length.

### Preserved interface and scope

All original independent variables remain in the reformulated model. The mapping script returns only those variables, restoring fixed endpoint coordinates from the unchanged self-contained data when necessary and excluding auxiliary variables.

The reformulation preserves the original obstacle exemptions, support requirements on inactive node slots, and absence of mandatory rack traversal or same-pipe self-avoidance. Its intended benefits are tighter linear relaxations and a more explicit MILP representation; no measured runtime improvement is established by the conversation.
